# Tier 2 Worked Solution, Topic 2.5

## The task

Add exactly one chart that answers "and what is driving it".

## The finding the chart must expose

| Branch | Leading product line | Its Sales |
|---|---|---|
| Ikeja | Home and lifestyle | ₦2,241,719.55 |
| Wuse | Sports and travel | ₦1,998,819.90 |
| Trans-Amadi | Food and beverages | ₦2,376,685.50 |

Three branches, three different leaders. This is invisible in the chain-wide product line chart from
Tier 1, where Food and beverages leads overall, and it is the actual answer to "what is driving it".

## Two acceptable answers

**A clustered bar chart of Product line by Branch**, built from the Topic 2.4 cross-tab. Cluster by
branch so each group holds six bars and the within-branch comparison is the one the eye makes. All
three branches are visible at once, which is the strength, and eighteen bars is the cost.

**A single-branch product line chart driven by the slicer.** Six bars at a time, far easier to read,
and the comparison across branches now depends on the reader clicking. Stronger as a display, weaker
as an argument, because a reader who does not click sees only one branch.

Both are full credit. What is marked is whether the reader can reach the finding without being told.

## The three sentences

A full-credit answer says something equivalent to:

> I added a clustered bar chart of Sales by Product line, grouped by branch, because the manager's
> second question is about what differs between branches and that needs both dimensions on one chart.
> I clustered by branch rather than by product line so that each group answers "what sells here",
> which is the question a stock decision actually turns on. I rejected adding a second pie chart and
> a table of all eighteen figures: the pie cannot rank values this close, and the table makes the
> reader do the comparison that the chart is supposed to do for them.

## Marking notes

- The chart exposing three different leaders: the substance.
- The rejection sentence: the part most trainees skip, and the part that shows the judgement being
  assessed. A dashboard is defined as much by what is left off it as by what goes on.
- Any submission that adds three or four charts here has missed the constraint. The brief says one.
