# Tier 3 Worked Solution, Topic 2.5

## Test 1, the cold question

**"Which branch is performing best, and what is driving it?"**

Expected: **Trans-Amadi**, at ₦11,056,870.65, driven by Food and beverages at
₦2,376,685.50. The reader should reach the first half from the top-left chart without clicking.

**"Which branch should we worry about?"** has no single right answer, and that is deliberate. Two
defensible readings, both verified:

| Answer | The case for it |
|---|---|
| **Wuse** | Lowest total Sales at ₦10,619,767.20, and the lowest average rating of the three at 6.82 against Ikeja's 7.03 and Trans-Amadi's 7.07 |
| **Ikeja** | Handles the most transactions of any branch, 340, and still does not lead on revenue, so its average basket is the weakest |

Either is full credit **with a figure attached**. A confident answer with no number behind it is not,
and this is the specific habit the test exists to build.

Note that a trainee's own cleaned file, with the nine blank Ratings left in place, will produce
average ratings very slightly different from the answer key's, which fills them. Both round to the
same picture and either is acceptable; what matters is that the trainee says which file they measured.

## Test 2, the slicer

Every button, including Ctrl multi-select, with every chart moving every time.

The disconnected-chart sentence must land on this:

> The dashboard carries on working and shows no error at all, so two charts show one branch while the
> third shows the whole chain, and nothing on the screen tells the reader that they are looking at
> two different questions answered side by side.

That is a sharper failure than a broken chart, because a broken chart gets noticed and fixed.

## Test 3, pie versus bar

Ewallet 345, Cash 344, Credit card 311. As shares of 1,000 transactions: 34.5, 34.4 and 31.1 per cent.

A full-credit answer says something equivalent to:

> I would send the bar chart with data labels. Ewallet leads Cash by a single transaction out of a
> thousand, and there is no size of pie chart on which a reader can see that, so the pie would
> invite them to guess at an order the data does not really support. The bar chart with labels shows
> the same three values and makes the honest point visible: these three payment methods are used
> almost equally, and the ranking between the top two is not worth acting on.

The last clause is the strongest thing a trainee can write here. Recognising that a difference is too
small to be worth reporting is a more advanced skill than ranking the values.

## Marking notes

- All three tests attempted and written up: the mechanical requirement.
- Figures attached to every claim: the assessed habit.
- A trainee who rearranges the dashboard as a result of test 1, and says what they moved and why, has
  used the test as it was intended rather than treating it as a formality.
