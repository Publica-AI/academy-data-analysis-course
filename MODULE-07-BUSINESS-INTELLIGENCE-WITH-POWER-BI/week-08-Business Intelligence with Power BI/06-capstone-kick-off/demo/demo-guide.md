# Demo Guide - Capstone Kick-off
**Module 7, Topic 7.6 | Estimated duration: 20-25 minutes**

---

## What This Demo Teaches

- Applying the "named decision maker" test live, to tell a genuine business question apart from a topic
- Using the sentence template to turn a vague idea into a testable question
- Walking through the four sign-off criteria against a real sample proposal, out loud, so trainees see the judgement process before applying it to their own
- Recognising the three dataset traps in a live example, not just from a description

> This demo has no software build and no code, unlike the rest of Module 7. The skill being demonstrated is judgement, applied live to worked examples, before trainees apply it to their own proposal.

---

## Setup - Before the Demo Starts

1. Three or four short, written sample proposal ideas ready on cards or a slide, at least one of which should clearly fail the checklist and at least one of which should clearly pass, prepared in advance rather than improvised
2. The blank sign-off form, `capstone-signoff-form.md` in this folder, printed or on screen so trainees see the exact fields before Part 3
3. Printed mentor assignments ready, not yet handed out

> **Instructor note:** at least one sample proposal should be a near-miss, not an obvious fail, since the near-miss is what most trainees will actually produce and the one they most need to see judged.

---

## Demo Steps

### Part 1 - The Named-Decision-Maker Test, Applied Live (7 min)

**Read a weak sample idea aloud:** "Analysis of Nigerian fintech transaction data."

> "Before I tell you what is wrong with this, run the test yourselves, silently. Could a named person act differently depending on the answer? Hands up if you can picture that person."

*(Few or no hands.)*

> "That is a topic, not a question. Now watch me fix it."

**Apply the sentence template live, narrating the choices:**

> "Named decision maker: a fraud analyst at a payments company. Type of organisation: a Nigerian fintech. What varies: the rate of failed transactions. Across what dimension: by bank and by time of day. What should they do about it: decide where to focus manual review."

**Read the result aloud:** "For a fraud analyst at a Nigerian fintech, how does the failed-transaction rate vary by bank and time of day, and where should manual review be focused?"

> "Same underlying interest. Now it names a person and a decision. That is the whole difference."

---

### Part 2 - Walking the Four Criteria Against a Sample (8 min)

**Present a second sample proposal, this time a near-miss:** a trainee proposes a beautifully clean, already-scored Kaggle competition dataset on customer churn, with a clear business question attached.

> "Let us run this through all four criteria, out loud, the way I will run yours in a moment."

**Real.** "Is it from a real source? Yes, Kaggle, properly licensed. Pass."

**Sufficient.** "Enough rows, more than one dimension to break it down by? Yes. Pass."

**No personal data.** "Check the columns. No names, no identifiers. Pass."

**Genuine question.** "Names a decision maker, a customer retention manager, and a measurable difference. Pass on paper."

> "Four passes. And I am still going to flag this one, because it is too clean. A pre-scored competition dataset has already had its cleaning problems solved by someone else. A large share of your marks come from demonstrating that work yourselves, and a spotless file gives you nothing to show. This is the too clean trap, and it can pass all four criteria and still be a weak choice."

---

### Part 3 - The Sign-Off Form, Field by Field (5 min)

**Display the blank form. Point at each field in turn.**

> "Business question, one sentence, using the template if it helps. Named decision maker, and what they would do differently. Dataset source, link, licence, row and column count. Your own answer to each of the four criteria, one line each."

> "Blank fields are not acceptable. If you genuinely do not have a dataset yet, write that honestly in the source field - your form comes back marked not yet, which is a completely normal outcome today."

---

## Common Student Issues During This Demo

| Issue | What to say |
|-------|-------------|
| A trainee insists their idea passes the named-decision-maker test but cannot say who the person is | "If you cannot picture the specific person, in a specific role, at a specific organisation, it has not passed yet. Push for a name and a job title, not a category of person." |
| A trainee argues the too clean trap is unfair because clean data should be rewarded | "It is rewarded, later, in how quickly you move to analysis. It is not rewarded at the sign-off stage, because the sign-off stage is checking whether there is a project here at all, not how easy it will be." |
| A trainee wants to submit with the dataset field left as 'TBD' | "That is a blank field, not an honest source entry. Ask them to write 'no dataset chosen yet, considering X and Y' instead - specific enough to act on, honest about where they actually are." |
